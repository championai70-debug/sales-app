    How the app decides
    
    
      
  1. Top sellers. A gradient-boosted tree model (the same idea as XGBoost) learns from your history how many units each article sells over your time frame, using recent sales, trend, price, margin, age and how its category is doing.
    2. 
      
  2. New articles. Articles without sales get the forecast of their three most similar articles (same category, close price, same brand or colour), reduced by 20% to be careful.
    3. 
      
  3. Sell-through. Units sold in the last 8 weeks divided by units sold plus stock now. Categories that sell through faster get a bigger share of the budget.
    4. 
      
  4. Euros per category. The budget is split by what each category needs to reach your target sell-through, weighted by its sell-through.
    5. 
      
  5. Units per article. Each category's euros go to its articles by need, a little more for top sellers and a little less for slow ones, then are turned into units at cost price.
    6. 
      
  6. Accuracy. The app hides your last weeks of sales, forecasts them from the weeks before, and compares. Whole plan and category numbers are what budgets rest on; single articles always swing more.
